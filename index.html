Core Research Pillars

Hardware Constraints Meet Algorithmic Rigor

Most machine learning benchmarks treat compute as unbounded and evaluation as aggregate cross-entropy. My research focuses on the two hardest failure modes: what breaks when you compress models to physical edge budgets, and what gets silenced when low-resource languages lack faithful representations.

1. Edge Compression & Trajectory Fidelity

Investigating the hidden failure modes of recursive reasoning under aggressive quantization, pruning, and linear attention.

  • The Illusion of Token Accuracy: Under naive INT4, cell accuracy remains deceptively high (88.6%) while puzzle-exact reasoning collapses to absolute 0.0%.
  • Carry-Trajectory Fidelity: Formulated a label-free cosine metric tracking latent state progression that forecasts reasoning collapse before full evaluation.
  • Physical Deployment: Flash-streamed embeddings bypass 99.4 MB SRAM bottlenecks; INT8 at single recursion cycles cuts FLOPs by 6× for 8 MB SoCs.
arXiv:2609.39277 & arXiv:2606.26488 · with Prof. Virginia Smith (CMU) & Steven Kolawole

2. Low-Resource African Speech & Tone

Building foundational acoustic models and sub-second pronunciation assessment for complex tonal languages like Yoruba and Hausa.

  • Representation Over Corpus Volume: Replacing 39 manual acoustic features with wav2vec2 lifted macro F1 from 0.432 to 0.811; BiGRU temporal modeling boosted tone accuracy to 79.6%.
  • Distilled Neural Aligner: Swapped MFA GMM-HMM for a 2 MB distilled neural head, slashing latency from 14.3s to 0.44s (4.7× realtime on CPU).
  • Cascading Pipeline Failure: Documented 95.6% translation collapse in educational ASR where tone error hit 100%.
Accepted at WiML @ ICML 2026 & ICBINB @ ICLR 2026

3. Modular Routing & Provable Agent Evaluation

Challenging parameter bloat through context-aware routing and eliminating hallucination via multi-tiered provenance harnesses.

  • Remix, Don’t Expand: Routing directly over a compact 64-dim base matched 512-dim baselines with 8× fewer active dimensions and 82.5% fewer parameters.
  • SEC 10-K Agent Harness: Benchmarked LangGraph vs Think-Act-Observe over 12 tasks with exact XBRL ground truth, catching silent fiscal year mismatch bugs.
  • Autonomy Calibration: In caseflow, calibrated confidence (ECE 0.029) lets systems auto-accept 43.9% of rulings at 90.8% accuracy.
Accepted at LM4UC @ AAAI 2026 · caseflow & SEC Harness

A Tilted Bowl Is Not a Slippery Slope: Compressing Looped Models

Steven Kolawole*, Pearse Jim*, Opegbemi M. Busoye, Glory Bagai, Virginia Smith (*Equal contribution)

ML Collective • Carnegie Mellon University (CMU)

Investigates the dynamics of aggressive compression in settling/looped architectures. Proves and empirically demonstrates that fixed rounding error does not accumulate indefinitely in settling loops; rather, it shifts the fixed point (akin to tilting a bowl shifting where a marble rests). Reveals that failed compressed models can recover accuracy with 8-bit finishing loops, and introduces a dynamic halting controller that outperforms fixed-depth execution by up to 15 points on Sudoku-Extreme and Maze-Hard while requiring only one-third of the weight traffic.

arXiv:2606.26488 Preprint (June 2026) Co-First Author

What Survives When You Compress a Recursive Reasoner for the Edge?

Pearse Jim*, Steven Kolawole*, Opegbemi M. Busoye, Glory Bagai, Virginia Smith (*Equal contribution)

ML Collective • Carnegie Mellon University (CMU)

Sweeps precision across three reasoning tasks and two recursive architectures to investigate edge deployment. Demonstrates that naive INT4 quantization deceptively maintains local token accuracy while silently collapsing puzzle-exact multi-step reasoning to zero. Introduces carry-trajectory fidelity, a label-free cosine diagnostic that predicts collapse, and formulates an end-to-end recipe: flash-streaming embeddings to overcome 99.4 MB SRAM bottlenecks, INT8 execution at single recursion cycles, and per-channel calibrated INT4 fitting a full reasoner onto a 4 MB microcontroller without retraining.

Why Large Language Models Fail for Hausa Educational Content: Cascading Errors from Translation to Speech to Comprehension

Pearse Jim (First Author)

ML Collective

Presents the first stratified evaluation of the complete English → Hausa → Speech → Comprehension pipeline for exam content, analyzing 790 balanced WASSCE exam questions over 10 disciplines and 15 years against five ASR models across human and TTS audio. Uncovers that standard aggregate Word Error Rate dangerously obscures systemic failures: subject ASR error varies significantly (54.6% Economics vs. 75.6% Chemistry, Kruskal-Wallis p < 10-6), a 63.4% WER precipitates a 95.6% translation fidelity collapse, and tonal/vowel-length transcription error rates reach 100%.

Remix, Don’t Expand: Context-Aware Embedding Routing

Pearse Jim

ML Collective

Challenges the paradigm of scaling monolithic embedding tables by applying Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing directly to the input embedding table rather than deep feed-forward blocks. Routing over a compact 64-dimensional base representation matches dense 512-dimensional baselines across five language modeling benchmarks with 8× fewer active dimensions and 82.5% fewer parameters (12.32M vs. 70.46M), demonstrating a 3.6× parameter-efficiency gain on Penn Treebank.
